# Fiction Evaluation Criteria

A practical rubric for assessing a work of fiction. Each section lists a criterion, the key questions an evaluator should ask, **positive signals** (what high-quality work looks like), and **red flags** (failure modes that justify a negative judgment).

The genre and intended audience determine which sections weigh most heavily — a literary novel is judged differently than a thriller, and a thriller differently than a cozy mystery. Always identify the book's genre contract before scoring.

---

## 1. Character

### 1.1 Protagonist Proactivity
- **Question:** Does the protagonist actively pursue a clearly defined goal, or do they merely react to events around them?
- **Positive signals:** A clear external want and internal need; choices that drive plot beats; agency over key turning points.
- **Red flags:** A passive protagonist whose arc is dictated by coincidence or by other characters' actions; goals that shift arbitrarily.

### 1.2 Character Arc
- **Question:** Does each major character fundamentally change their worldview, behavior, or external circumstance as a result of the plot's conflicts?
- **Positive signals:** A measurable before/after state; internal change that mirrors or counterpoints external plot; flaws that are confronted, not merely stated.
- **Red flags:** Static characters in a story that demands change; "growth" that is told rather than dramatized; transformation untethered from the events of the plot.

### 1.3 Cast Differentiation
- **Question:** Are characters distinct in voice, motivation, and function?
- **Positive signals:** Characters identifiable by cadence and vocabulary alone; each cast member serves a unique narrative role.
- **Red flags:** An oversized cast; interchangeable voices; characters who exist only to deliver exposition or convenience.

### 1.4 Character-Plot Integration
- **Question:** Is narrative momentum fueled by character flaws, desires, and psychology — or by external mechanics alone?
- **Positive signals:** Plot consequences flow directly from character decisions; antagonists are shaped by the protagonist's specific weakness.
- **Red flags:** Plot would proceed identically with different characters; characters are vehicles for plot machinery rather than its source.

## 2. Plot

### 2.1 Opening Hook
- **Question:** Does the opening capture attention while establishing setting, tone, and stakes?
- **Positive signals:** A specific entry point that raises a question the reader needs answered; tone and genre are signaled within the first pages.
- **Red flags:** Prologue-as-info-dump; slow, atmospheric opening with no implicit question; tonal mismatch with the rest of the book.

### 2.2 Plot Logic & Coherence
- **Question:** Do twists and resolutions stem logically from prior events?
- **Positive signals:** Reveals feel inevitable in hindsight; foreshadowing pays off; the story "earns" its turns.
- **Red flags:** Plot holes; contradictions; deus ex machina; twists that require the reader to forget earlier scenes.

### 2.3 Obligatory Scenes
- **Question:** Does the book deliver the specific scenes its premise and genre promise?
- **Positive signals:** Final confrontation between protagonist and primary antagonist; genre-mandated beats are present and emotionally weighted (e.g., lovers reunite in romance; killer revealed in mystery; final battle in fantasy).
- **Red flags:** Missing climactic scenes; payoffs skipped or rushed past; the book ends before it pays what its premise promised.

### 2.4 Stakes
- **Question:** Are the stakes — internal, interpersonal, and external — clear and high enough to sustain interest?
- **Positive signals:** What the protagonist will lose if they fail is concrete and emotionally felt; stakes escalate over the course of the book.
- **Red flags:** Vague threats; stakes that are stated but never dramatized; static stakes across all three acts.

### 2.5 Chapter Necessity
- **Question:** Can any chapter be removed without the narrative collapsing?
- **Positive signals:** Every chapter advances plot, character, or thematic argument — ideally more than one at a time.
- **Red flags:** Detachable scenes; chapters that exist for atmosphere alone; redundant beats.

---

## 3. Pacing

### 3.1 Ebb and Flow
- **Question:** Does narrative speed match the demands of each moment?
- **Positive signals:** Acceleration during action and climactic beats; deliberate slowdowns for character development; tension rises and releases in a controlled rhythm.
- **Red flags:** Uniform pacing across the book; stalled middles; rushed endings; "breathing room" that turns into wandering.

### 3.2 Scene-Level Pacing
- **Question:** Within scenes, does each beat earn its space?
- **Positive signals:** Scenes enter late and leave early; descriptive passages serve mood or plot rather than padding.
- **Red flags:** Lingering setup; excessive interiority that halts forward motion; filler description.

---

## 4. Dialogue

### 4.1 Purpose
- **Question:** Does every exchange reveal character, advance plot, or surface conflict?
- **Positive signals:** Lines do double duty; conversations leave the situation different than they found it.
- **Red flags:** Small talk for its own sake; redundant beats that repeat what the reader already knows.

### 4.2 Subtext
- **Question:** Do characters say what they mean — or do they speak around it?
- **Positive signals:** Meaning is conveyed through evasion, deflection, and implication; the reader feels the gap between what is said and what is meant.
- **Red flags:** Characters announce their feelings directly; emotional beats are stated rather than implied.

### 4.3 Exposition Discipline
- **Question:** Is backstory or world information smuggled in through dialogue?
- **Positive signals:** Information emerges naturally from conflict or curiosity.
- **Red flags:** "As you know, Bob…" dialogue; characters explain things they would never explain in real life.

### 4.4 Voice Differentiation
- **Question:** Can characters be identified by cadence, vocabulary, and rhythm without dialogue tags?
- **Positive signals:** Distinct speech patterns aligned with character background and psychology.
- **Red flags:** All characters speak in the author's voice; tags are required to track who's talking.

### 4.5 Compression
- **Question:** Is dialogue tight, or is it cluttered with realistic filler?
- **Positive signals:** Lines are sharpened past realism; hedges and verbal tics are used sparingly and meaningfully.
- **Red flags:** Excessive "um," "well," "I mean"; meandering exchanges that mirror real life rather than dramatize it.

---

## 5. Prose & Style

### 5.1 Sentence-Level Craft
- **Question:** Are word choices precise, rhythms controlled, and imagery active?
- **Positive signals:** Verbs do the work; specific nouns over abstractions; sentence variety matches scene energy.
- **Red flags:** Adverb-heavy prose; clichés in load-bearing positions; recurring grammatical issues.

### 5.2 Voice
- **Question:** Does the narrator have a discernible, consistent personality?
- **Positive signals:** A distinctive sensibility carries through; tonal control across scenes.
- **Red flags:** Generic, interchangeable prose; voice that drifts between chapters.

### 5.3 Close-Reading Integrity (literary fiction)
- **Question:** Does the text hold up to sentence-by-sentence scrutiny? (the New Critical lens)
- **Positive signals:** Metaphor, irony, tension, and paradox cohere into a unified work; recurring imagery accrues meaning.
- **Red flags:** Decorative figurative language that doesn't connect to theme; ironies that read as accidents.

## 6. Genre Contract (Five-Leaf Clover)

Identify and evaluate the book against each of the five dimensions of Shawn Coyne's *Story Grid* framework. The genre is a contract; the reader's expectations are not optional.

| Dimension | What to Evaluate | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Plot Type** | Does the book deliver the obstacles its category promises? | Action, Horror, Romance, Coming-of-Age, Machiavellian |
| **Structure** | Does the narrative framework fit the story being told? | Archplot (three-act), Miniplot (circular), Antiplot (postmodern) |
| **Distance from Reality** | Is suspension of disbelief calibrated and held? | Contemporary, Historical, Alternate History, Magical Realism, Fantasy |
| **Style** | Does artistic execution serve the material? | Epistolary, Minimalist, Cinematic, Theatrical, Satirical |
| **Content / Length** | Does the physical length suit the thematic scope? | Short Story, Novella, Novel, Epic |

- **Positive signals:** All five dimensions reinforce one another; the book "knows what it is."
- **Red flags:** Marketing/genre signals that the book does not deliver on; structural choices that fight the material.

## 7. World-Building (Speculative / Historical)

### 7.1 Rule Consistency
- **Question:** Are the cultural, social, magical, technological, or historical rules of the setting consistent throughout?
- **Positive signals:** Rules established early are honored under pressure; new constraints integrate cleanly.
- **Red flags:** Sudden, unexplained changes in world logic; magic or tech that conveniently solves whatever the plot needs.

### 7.2 Specificity
- **Question:** Is the world rendered through specific, telling detail rather than generic furniture?
- **Positive signals:** Cultural texture emerges through food, custom, language, and economy; the setting affects how characters think.
- **Red flags:** Interchangeable "fantasy kingdom" or "futuristic city" defaults; world details that never bear on plot or character.

### 7.3 Integration
- **Question:** Does the world produce the story, or is the world a backdrop to a story that could have happened anywhere?
- **Positive signals:** The plot could not exist outside this specific setting.
- **Red flags:** Setting is removable without affecting the narrative.

---

## 8. Emotional & Thematic Resonance

### 8.1 Sublime (Longinian) Test
- **Question:** Does the work transport the reader — produce awe, grief, fear, joy, recognition — beyond surface engagement?
- **Positive signals:** Specific scenes lodge in memory; the reader feels altered.
- **Red flags:** Competent execution without resonance; affective beats that read as mechanical.

### 8.2 Thematic Coherence
- **Question:** Does the book have an argument — about people, the world, or experience — that emerges from its events?
- **Positive signals:** Theme is enacted through plot and character rather than stated; multiple scenes refract the same underlying question.
- **Red flags:** Theme is announced in dialogue or epigraph; "messaging" that overrides the story.

---

## 9. Presentation & Mechanics

### 9.1 Technical Proficiency
- **Question:** Is the manuscript free of recurring grammatical, structural, or copy-editing failures?
- **Positive signals:** Clean, professional prose; consistent formatting.
- **Red flags:** Persistent errors that signal an unfinished draft.

### 9.2 Design
- **Question:** Do cover, interior layout, and typography meet professional standards?
- **Positive signals:** Visual presentation signals credibility; layout supports readability.
- **Red flags:** Amateurish cover; cramped or inconsistent interior.

---

## Synthesis: Overall Assessment

When all sections are scored, answer these final questions before issuing a verdict:

1. **Does the book deliver its genre contract?**
2. **Is the protagonist's arc fundamentally connected to the plot's resolution?**
3. **Does the prose serve the story, or does the story serve the prose?**
4. **Would a chapter, a character, or a subplot be removable without loss? If yes, that's a structural failure.**
5. **Will any specific scene or image stay with the reader a month later?** (The Longinian test)
6. **What would change if a different writer of similar skill had told this story?** (If the answer is "nothing," voice is missing.)

Maintain the **60/40 reading stance** (Edward Weeks): 60% of attention surrenders to the narrative; 40% remains detached and observant of mechanics. Refuse generic superlatives ("thrilling," "intriguing") in favor of specific, evidence-backed judgments.

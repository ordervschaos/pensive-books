# The Character Engine: Ghost, Lie, Want, Need

**What it is:** A four-part psychological formula for building a protagonist whose internal flaw the plot will systematically attack and dismantle. It turns "who is this person?" into a mechanism that *generates* plot.

**Why it matters:** A [structure](../README.md) is an empty vessel. This is the engine that fills it — the plot is not a sequence of events but a machine engineered to test the protagonist's false belief until it shatters. Build this *before or alongside* your structure; nearly every structure here (Hero's Journey, Save the Cat, Romancing the Beat) assumes it.

---

## The four pillars

1. **The Ghost** (a.k.a. the Wound, the Origin Scene). A significant, often traumatic past event that shattered the character's innocent worldview. Fiction distills this to *one* potent incident even though real people are shaped by thousands. The "wound" is the ongoing emotional damage it left behind.
2. **The Lie** (the Misbelief). To survive the Ghost, the character drew a wrong conclusion — a self-limiting philosophy that is factually false but emotionally protective.
   - *Frozen*: Elsa harms her sister (Ghost) → "I'm dangerous and must isolate myself" (Lie).
   - *I, Robot*: Spooner watches a robot save him over a child by survival odds (Ghost) → "robots are inherently malevolent" (Lie).
3. **The Want** (the Goal). The character's external pursuit, driven by the Lie — they falsely believe achieving it will heal the wound.
4. **The Need** (the Truth). The psychological antidote to the Lie. The climax forces the character to a breaking point: the Want is meaningless or unattainable unless they abandon the Lie and embrace the painful Truth.

---

## How to apply it

- Work backward from the Lie. Once you know the false belief, the Ghost (what caused it) and the Need (what disproves it) fall into place — and the plot becomes "a series of events that pressure-test this Lie."
- Map the Want against the Need. The richest tension comes when what the character *wants* is at odds with what they *need*. The arc is the journey from one to the other.
- Decide the arc's polarity:
  - **Positive arc** — the character sheds the Lie and embraces the Truth.
  - **Tragic arc** — the character clings to the Lie despite the evidence, leading to their downfall (physical, mental, or professional).

## Pitfalls

- **A stated wound, not a dramatized one.** The Ghost should manifest in behavior, not be explained in a monologue.
- **A Lie the plot never tests.** If events don't repeatedly press on the false belief, the character won't change and the structure will feel hollow.
- **Diffuse trauma.** Many small backstory hurts dilute impact; distill to the single load-bearing incident.

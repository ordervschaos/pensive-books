# Hooks: Openings & Chapter Endings

**What it is:** The craft of capturing and holding attention at the micro level. A hook isn't a shocking event — it's a *psychological promise* to the reader, made by raising an unanswered question.

**Why it matters:** Structure governs macro-pacing; hooks govern whether the reader turns the next page. A perfectly structured book that opens flat is never read far enough for the structure to matter. Hooks operate at two scales: the opening, and the start/end of every chapter.

**Pairs with:** Any [structure](../README.md). The opening hook is the first beat of whatever blueprint you're using (e.g., Save the Cat's "Opening Image," the Fichtean Curve's in-medias-res crisis).

---

## Types of opening hook

Each works by introducing a question the reader needs answered:

- **The "Why" Hook.** A baffling situation that demands immediate explanation.
- **The "Character" Hook.** Instant empathy or morbid curiosity toward the protagonist's extreme behavior, voice, or logic.
- **The "Catastrophe" Hook.** Open directly in high-stakes action or physical peril.
- **The "Contradicting Emotions" Hook.** A character reacting inappropriately (laughing at a funeral) — the mismatch creates intrigue.
- **The "Inherent Problem" Hook.** Immediately present the systemic imbalance or obstacle in the character's ordinary world.

## What the opening chapter must accomplish

Without becoming a dense info-dump, the first chapter should:

- Establish the **"before" state** of the character's arc — implicitly show their [Ghost and Lie](character-engine.md).
- Ground the reader in setting, time period, and **genre tone** within the first few paragraphs.
- Pose the immediate plot question and the stakes of failure.
- End on the character making a **proactive choice** — not remaining passive.

## Micro-hooks: every chapter

The hook is not a one-time event on page one. Build a hook at both ends of every chapter:

- **Chapter opening:** re-engage curiosity and establish localized tension.
- **Chapter ending:** act as a springboard — raise a new complication, introduce a fresh mystery, or land a cliffhanger that overrides the reader's impulse to put the book down.

---

## How to apply it

- Pick the opening hook type that matches your genre's contract: a thriller wants Catastrophe or Why; a literary novel may want Character or Contradicting Emotions.
- Audit chapter endings in revision. If a chapter ends on resolution rather than a new question, the reader has permission to stop — give them a reason not to.
- Let the hook raise a question you actually intend to answer. A hook that writes a check the book doesn't cash erodes trust.

## Pitfalls

- **Prologue-as-info-dump.** Front-loading backstory in the name of "setup" buries the hook.
- **A passive opening.** Atmosphere with no implicit question gives the reader nothing to chase.
- **Resolved chapter endings.** Tidy closure mid-book repeatedly invites the reader to leave.

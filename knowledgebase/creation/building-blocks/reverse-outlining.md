# Reverse Outlining

**What it is:** An editorial diagnostic that builds an outline *from a finished draft* — the opposite of planning. You distill what you actually wrote into a skeletal x-ray of the book's real architecture, then read that map to find structural flaws the prose was hiding.

**Why it matters:** It shifts you from creative generation to critical evaluation. Engaging prose camouflages pacing lags, logical gaps, and tangents — the reverse outline strips the prose away so those flaws become visible. For [discovery writers](../approaches/discovery-writing.md), this is the paramount, non-negotiable tool: it imposes structure retroactively without sacrificing the spontaneity of the draft.

**When in the process:** After drafting, before line editing. Also the second half of two [hybrid](../approaches/hybrid-drafting.md) workflows (zero-draft → reverse outline).

---

## The process

1. **Separate from the draft.** Approach the finished (or near-finished) draft with fresh eyes — ideally after an incubation period long enough to forget your original intentions and read objectively.
2. **Distill each paragraph.** Moving through the document, write one condensed sentence (~4–10 words) per paragraph summarizing its core action or "unit of thought." *If you can't summarize a paragraph concisely, it lacks focus or is doing too much.*
3. **Analyze function.** Next to each summary, note *how* that paragraph advances the macro plot, theme, or argument. Does it establish character? Raise tension? Deliver necessary exposition?
4. **Read the skeleton.** The list of sentences is your book's true architecture. Reading only the outline exposes:
   - **Pacing lags** — sequential paragraphs hitting the same beat or repeating exposition.
   - **Logical gaps** — missing causal links where the narrative leaps without justification.
   - **Tangent drift** — summaries that don't align with the chapter's thesis or the scene's drive.
5. **Reorganize.** Color-code and rearrange: regroup topics, split bloated sections, rewrite weak topic sentences, and move misplaced material into a "holding" document for later integration.

---

## How to apply it

- Do the distillation in a *separate* clean document, not the margins, if you want to read the skeleton uninterrupted by your own prose.
- Be ruthless in step 2. The inability to summarize is the signal — don't smooth it over; flag the paragraph.
- Use step 3 to catch chapters that read fine but do nothing: if a paragraph's only function is "atmosphere," question whether it earns its place.

## Pitfalls

- **Skipping the incubation.** Reverse-outlining while still attached to your intentions means you'll summarize what you *meant*, not what's on the page.
- **Summarizing generously.** "Stuff happens, it's tense" hides exactly the problem the technique exists to surface. Force the concrete unit of thought.

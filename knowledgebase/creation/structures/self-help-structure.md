# The Self-Help Framework

**What it is:** A guided curriculum designed to facilitate a specific transformation in the reader. It swaps the fictional character arc for the *reader's* journey from problem to changed behavior, and runs on two nested structures: a five-part book arc and a per-chapter engagement loop.

**Best for:** Prescriptive non-fiction — books that promise the reader will *do* or *become* something. Choose this over [memoir](memoir-structure.md) when the value is instruction, not personal story.

**Approach it pairs with:** [Outlining](../approaches/outlining.md). Self-help is argument architecture; plan it before drafting.

---

## The five-part book structure (macro)

A reliable map from the reader's pain to their changed life:

1. **Problem, Premise, Promise.** Name the reader's urgent pain point, establish your empathy and expertise, and map the journey from problem to solution.
2. **History of the Problem.** Deconstruct the psychological, historical, or systemic roots — so the reader understands *why* they've been struggling.
3. **Setup for Action.** Prepare the reader psychologically; shift their mindset before asking them to act.
4. **Take Action.** The core strategies, behaviors, and concrete steps.
5. **Troubleshooting and Future.** Sustaining the change — handling edge cases, relapse, and integrating the new mindset into daily life and community.

## The chapter engine: Hook–Book–Look–Took (micro)

Run this loop in each chapter so the reader stays engaged *and* able to execute:

- **Hook.** Open with a story, a probing question, or a surprising statistic that creates emotional investment in the chapter's topic.
- **Book (the concept).** Explain one focused idea clearly, without overwhelming the reader.
- **Look (the proof).** A case study or anecdote demonstrating the concept works.
- **Took (the application).** Step-by-step instructions specific enough to act on immediately.

---

## How to use it to create a book

- Define the **proto-persona** first: one specific reader, with exact pain points and barriers. "For everyone" is the most common failure — a precise reader makes every chapter sharper.
- Keep one idea per chapter. The Hook–Book–Look–Took loop breaks down the moment a chapter tries to teach two things at once.
- The **Took** is what separates a transformative book from a motivational one: instructions must be concrete enough that the reader can execute today.

## Pitfalls

- **Concept without application.** Insight that the reader can't act on produces a pleasant read and zero change — and self-help is judged on change.
- **Audience too broad.** Targeting a 75-million-person demographic ("Baby Boomers") makes the book unfocused and commercially weak.
- **Emotion substituting for evidence.** Pair claims with proof (the Look); unsupported assertion reads as a platitude.

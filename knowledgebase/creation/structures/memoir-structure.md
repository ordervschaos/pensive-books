# The Memoir Framework

**What it is:** A structure for non-fiction built around a curated personal transformation — not a chronological record of a life. A memoir explores a single theme, timeframe, or change; an autobiography or diary recounts events.

**Best for:** Personal narrative non-fiction. Choose this when the book's value is *your* lived experience shaped into meaning, rather than instruction (for that, see [self-help](self-help-structure.md)).

**Approach it pairs with:** Often [hybrid](../approaches/hybrid-drafting.md) — a discovery "zero draft" to find the emotional truth, then a [reverse outline](../building-blocks/reverse-outlining.md) to impose theme and shape.

---

## Two memoir types

Identify which you're writing — it changes how you generate narrative drama.

- **Quest memoir.** A physical action maps neatly onto emotional growth (overcoming addiction, climbing a mountain, a journey). The external arc carries the internal one.
- **Quiet memoir.** The change is internal and subtle, with no obvious external action. The challenge is presenting interior shifts *as* dramatic narrative.

## The two selves

The engine of memoir is the gap between two versions of you:

- **The "Character of You"** — your past self, navigating the events blindly, without hindsight.
- **The "Writer You"** — your present, knowing self, structuring those events to maximize meaning.

Your job is to invest past moments with deeper meaning than they held at the time — without letting the Writer You's hindsight rob the Character of You of suspense.

## The structural arc

A typical memoir outline tracks:

1. **Opening situation** — the moment that frames the theme.
2. **Childhood / context** — establishes the narrator's baseline self.
3. **Escalating hurdles** — obstacles that test and reshape the narrator.
4. **Dark night of the soul** — the lowest point.
5. **Resolution** — a clear demonstration of the narrator's transformation.

---

## How to use it to create a book

- Pick your **theme before your timeline.** A memoir is the slice of your life that serves one idea — everything outside that slice is cut.
- For a Quiet memoir, externalize: find the scenes, objects, and conversations that *show* the internal shift instead of narrating it.
- Decide consciously how much the Writer You intrudes. Too much hindsight kills tension; too little and the events feel meaningless.

## Pitfalls

- **The diary trap.** Chronological "and then, and then" with no thematic spine reads as journaling, not memoir.
- **Score-settling or self-flattery.** The Character of You must be allowed real flaws, or the reader won't trust the transformation.

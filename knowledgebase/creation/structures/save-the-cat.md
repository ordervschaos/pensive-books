# Save the Cat! Beat Sheet

**What it is:** A percentage-based structural map of 15 specific "beats" that land at precise points in the manuscript to control pacing and emotional payoff. Created for screenplays by Blake Snyder; adapted for novelists by Jessica Brody.

**Best for:** Commercial fiction and any writer who wants tight, reliable pacing. The percentage markers turn "where am I structurally?" into a measurable question.

**Approach it pairs with:** [Outlining](../approaches/outlining.md). The beats double as an outline scaffold.

---

## The 15 beats

Percentages are of total manuscript length. Use them as targets, not laws.

| # | Beat | Location | Function |
|---|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Opening Image** | 0–1% | The "before" state; shows the protagonist's flaw and the tone. |
| 2 | **Theme Stated** | ~5% | A minor character hints at the truth the protagonist must learn. |
| 3 | **Setup** | 1–10% | Stakes, world, and the protagonist's reluctance to change. |
| 4 | **Catalyst** | ~10% | The inciting incident shatters the ordinary world. |
| 5 | **Debate** | 10–20% | The protagonist resists and doubts — a realistic aversion to change. |
| 6 | **Break Into 2** | ~20% | A proactive choice to enter the Act II world. |
| 7 | **B Story** | ~22% | A secondary subplot (often romance/mentorship) that carries the theme. |
| 8 | **Fun and Games** | 20–50% | The "promise of the premise" — the entertainment the genre promised. |
| 9 | **Midpoint** | ~50% | A false victory or false defeat that raises the stakes. |
| 10 | **Bad Guys Close In** | 50–75% | Internal flaws and external foes pile on compounding pressure. |
| 11 | **All Is Lost** | ~75% | The lowest point; a "whiff of death." |
| 12 | **Dark Night of the Soul** | 75–80% | The protagonist mourns, then sees the Lie is destroying them. |
| 13 | **Break Into 3** | ~80% | Armed with the truth, they form a final plan. |
| 14 | **Finale** | 80–99% | The climax — they apply the truth to win and prove they've changed. |
| 15 | **Final Image** | 99–100% | The inverse of the Opening Image; transformation made visible. |

---

## How to use it to create a book

- Compute your targets up front: for an 80,000-word novel, the Midpoint sits around word 40,000, All Is Lost around 60,000.
- **Theme Stated (5%)** and **Final Image (100%)** bookend the change arc. Write them as a pair so the ending answers the opening.
- Treat **Fun and Games** as the section readers actually came for — it must deliver the genre's core appeal, not just bridge two plot points.

## Pitfalls

- **Beat-checking instead of storytelling.** Beats are a diagnostic for pacing, not a substitute for causality between scenes.
- **Mechanical Midpoint.** A false victory/defeat only works if it genuinely reframes the stakes; don't insert a twist for the percentage's sake.

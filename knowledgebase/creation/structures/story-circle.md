# Dan Harmon's Story Circle

**What it is:** A stripped-down, eight-step version of the monomyth that emphasizes the symmetry of going out and coming back — and the price paid for change. Popularized by screenwriter Dan Harmon.

**Best for:** Character-driven stories, episodic or screen-style narratives, and any writer who finds the 12-stage Hero's Journey too elaborate. The cyclical shape makes it easy to hold in your head.

**Approach it pairs with:** Any. Light enough to guide [discovery writing](../approaches/discovery-writing.md) or anchor an [outline](../approaches/outlining.md).

---

## The 8 steps

Picture a circle cut in half: the top half is the familiar world, the bottom half the unfamiliar. Steps 1–4 descend; steps 5–8 climb back.

1. **You.** A character in a zone of comfort — ordinary, unchallenged life.
2. **Need.** They want something; a void or desire pushes them toward the edge of that comfort.
3. **Go.** They cross the threshold into an unfamiliar situation to pursue it.
4. **Search.** They adapt to the chaos of the new world, acquiring what they need to survive.
5. **Find.** They get what they were after.
6. **Take.** The pivotal beat — they pay a heavy, often unexpected price for it. No easy victory.
7. **Return.** They come back to the familiar world, carrying what they found.
8. **Change.** They're fundamentally altered; the old comfort zone no longer fits.

---

## How to use it to create a book

- The horizontal axis is **comfort ↔ chaos**; the vertical is **what they want vs. what they have.** Every step moves along both.
- **Step 6 (Take)** is the load-bearing beat. If achieving the goal costs nothing, the circle doesn't turn — and the ending won't land.
- Steps mirror across the circle (1↔8, 2↔7, 3↔6, 4↔5). Use that symmetry to check whether your ending answers your opening.

## Pitfalls

- **Superimposed formula.** Harmon's own rule: the plot must arise from the character's desire, not feel bolted on. If a step feels arbitrary, the Need (step 2) is too weak.
- **No real change.** If step 8 returns the character unchanged, you've drawn a circle that didn't move.

# Outlining (Plotting)

**What it is:** Building the architectural blueprint of the narrative *before* writing prose. You front-load the structural, logical, and character decisions so errors are caught in the plan, not the draft.

**Who it's for:** Writers who want to cut revision time (often from months to weeks), who are juggling complex plots — hard magic systems, deep mysteries, interlocking timelines — or who find the blank page paralyzing. If structure calms you, outline.

**Pairs with:** Any [structure](../README.md) — but especially [Snowflake](../structures/snowflake-method.md) and [Save the Cat](../structures/save-the-cat.md), which *are* outlining disciplines.

---

## The spectrum of outlining techniques

Outlining isn't one thing. Pick the granularity that matches your story's complexity and your tolerance for planning.

| Technique | What it is | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Foolscap Method** | The whole arc — beginning, middle, end — on a single page (Shawn Coyne). | A structural safety net while keeping scene-level freedom. |
| **Brief / Expanded Outline** | A few lines or a paragraph per scene. The format agents and publishers expect. | Balancing structure with flexibility; seeing the sequence without scripting it. |
| **Extensive / Detailed Outline** | Up to ~150 pages, every plot element tracked, margins for research. | Highly complex narratives — forensic thrillers where every clue must track. |
| **Chapter-by-Chapter Paragraphing** | A paragraph per chapter: setting, emotional payload, mechanical function. | Reading the "book" in summary to test pacing before writing prose. |
| **Index Cards / Spatial Plotting** | One scene per card, rearranged on a wall or in Scrivener/Plottr. | Multiple timelines or ensemble casts; reorganizing subplots by dragging. |
| **Spreadsheet / Matrix** | A grid — chapters down one axis, character arcs or clues across the other. | Continuity-heavy genres: intricate mysteries, high-fantasy epics. |

---

## How to use it to create a book

- Match granularity to risk. A character study can run on a Foolscap page; a clue-dependent mystery wants a spreadsheet.
- Outline at the level you'll actually maintain. An outline you abandon mid-draft helped less than a lighter one you kept current.
- Build characters and world *alongside* the plot outline — the [character engine](../building-blocks/character-engine.md) and [world rules](../building-blocks/world-building.md) generate plot, so outline them together.

## Strengths

- A tight, compelling structure that drives reader engagement from page one.
- Dead-ends, plot holes, and pacing problems surface in the plan, where they're cheap to fix.

## Pitfalls

- **Outlining yourself into a corner.** A rigidly perfect plan collapses when a character organically evolves. Stay flexible — adapt the outline rather than fighting the draft.
- **Planning as procrastination.** At some point the outline is a way to avoid writing. Set a "start drafting" trigger.

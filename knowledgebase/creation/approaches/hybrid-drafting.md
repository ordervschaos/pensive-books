# Hybrid Drafting

**What it is:** A synthesis of [outlining](outlining.md) and [discovery writing](discovery-writing.md) that resolves the false choice between structure and spontaneity. As Brandon Sanderson observes, *all* writers discover — the only question is *when.* Outliners "pants" the outline; pure pantsers "pants" the prose. Hybrid writers deliberately split the difference.

**Who it's for:** Most working authors. If neither pure planning nor pure improvisation fits cleanly, you're a hybrid — and that's the norm, not a compromise.

**Pairs with:** Any [structure](../README.md), applied at whatever stage you switch from discovery to planning.

---

## The patterns

Pick the one that matches where your uncertainty lives:

1. **Zero draft → reverse outline.** Write a fast, exploratory "zero draft" purely to find the story's themes, settings, and character voices. Then build a [reverse outline](../building-blocks/reverse-outlining.md) from it to construct a logical plot map, which becomes the rigid guide for the *real* first draft.
2. **Discover Act I → outline Acts II–III.** Discovery-write the first act to establish world and characters organically. Once the variables and mysteries are set, switch to a rigid outline to ensure a cohesive resolution.
3. **Loose mental outline + reverse outline.** Keep broad, fuzzy ideas mulling for a long time, draft from that loose sense, then reverse-outline afterward to reorganize heavily and fix plot discrepancies.

---

## How to use it to create a book

- Decide *where* your discovery should happen. If you don't know your characters' voices, discover Act I. If you don't know your structure, zero-draft then reverse-outline.
- Treat the switch from discovery to outline as a deliberate gear change, not a failure of planning. Pattern 2 exists precisely because some things can only be learned by writing them.
- The zero draft is disposable by design. Its job is to *teach you the story*, not to survive into the final book.

## Strengths

- Keeps the spontaneity and authentic voice of discovery writing while importing the structural integrity of outlining.
- Lets you spend planning effort only where you're actually uncertain.

## Pitfalls

- **Never leaving discovery mode.** Pattern 1 and 2 both require an honest switch to planning; skipping it just makes you a pantser with extra steps.
- **Over-revising the zero draft.** Polishing exploratory prose you'll discard wastes the time the method was meant to save.
